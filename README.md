# DamnMIT

**MIT. Because apparently "use, copy, modify" needed further clarification.**

I found the ordinary MIT License too general. What is "software"? What counts as a "copy"? Where exactly is "above" when the monitor is upside down? So I expanded the license itself into thousands of lines of definitions, scenarios, and clarifications of the clarifications.

There is no useful reason for this project to exist. It is bureaucratic trolling with an actual permission grant.

## The license

[LICENSE](LICENSE) is a custom, excessively expanded restatement of MIT-style permissions, its notice condition, and its warranty and liability disclaimers. The explanations are woven into the provisions themselves.

It is drafted to preserve MIT's substance: use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, and sell; retain the applicable copyright and permission notices; no warranty. The principal provisions and the MIT-consistent construction rule take precedence over contrary implications in examples.

**You do not have to redistribute the entire bureaucratic document.** Section 5.N1 expressly permits the full standard MIT text with the applicable copyright notices as sufficient notice preservation.

The wording is custom and has not undergone professional legal review. Equivalence to MIT and enforceability in any particular jurisdiction are not certified. DamnMIT has no claimed OSI approval or registered SPDX identifier.

## Use it yourself

You may copy and adapt the text for your project. Identify the material covered and use the appropriate copyright notices for your own work, preserving applicable upstream notices for material you reuse. Changing a template does not change the license of someone else's existing code.

Identify this custom text as **DamnMIT**, not unmodified SPDX `MIT`. Where custom SPDX references are supported, use `LicenseRef-DamnMIT` and provide the full text. If you want standard MIT wording and recognition, use [standard MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

## Administrative achievements

- 50 defined terms.
- 120 permission/context scenarios.
- 48 further clarifications about words such as "hereby", "any", and "above".
- An express provision preventing additional obligations arising solely from reader fatigue.
- No intended restrictions beyond MIT's notice condition.

The standard MIT License takes about a minute to read. We have addressed this deficiency.
